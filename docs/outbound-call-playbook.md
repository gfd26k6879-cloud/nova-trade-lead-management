# NoSite Leads Outbound Call Playbook

This is a practical first-call playbook for selling local websites to businesses that appear to have no website, a social-only presence, a basic site, or a broken/weak site.

The position for tomorrow is simple:

> I am an independent web designer helping a small number of local businesses turn their Google presence into a simple, professional website that gets more calls.

Do not present as a registered company if that is not true. Do not say "we are an agency" unless you have actually formed one. The strongest version is honest: "I am independent", "I am taking on a few first clients", "I can show you a free preview before you decide."

## Operating Rules

These rules are conservative. They are not legal advice, but they keep the first calling day away from obvious trouble.

- Manually dial public business numbers only. Do not use robocalls, prerecorded messages, AI voice, autodialers, or cold marketing texts.
- If someone says "do not call", stop, apologize, and mark the lead as do-not-call.
- Do not call personal numbers found through data brokers. Use the number listed on Google Business Profile, the business site, or the business directory record.
- Do not claim partnership with Google, Yelp, Facebook, the city, or any directory.
- Do not say you were referred unless you were actually referred.
- Do not take card details over the phone. Close for a short meeting, preview review, written scope, invoice, and then payment.
- Before scaling Colorado calls, verify whether you need Colorado telemarketing registration or a claimed exemption. Colorado AG guidance says a commercial telephone seller contacting Colorado purchasers may need to register at least ten days before doing business in the state.

## The Psychology That Should Shape Every Call

Use these as ethical levers, not manipulation.

- Specificity beats hype. Mention the exact business, city, category, rating/reviews, and website issue.
- Reciprocity: give something useful before asking for money. The useful thing is a free mini-audit or preview.
- Autonomy: make it easy to say no. "If this is not useful, no problem." This lowers resistance.
- Loss aversion: frame the website gap as missed calls from people already searching, not as "your business is bad."
- Social proof: if you do not have client proof yet, do not fake it. Use category proof instead: "I am focusing on local service businesses where calls matter."
- Commitment and consistency: ask for small yeses first. "Is this still the right number?" "Are you the person who handles the website?" "Would it be useful if I sent a quick preview?"
- Authority: sound prepared. A calm, specific observation beats credentials you do not have.
- Liking and similarity: be plainspoken. "I am local/independent and I am trying to build a small client base" is better than agency jargon.
- Anchoring: compare the cost to one customer or one job, not abstract design value. For plumbers, roofers, electricians, dentists, med spas, lawyers, and auto shops, one extra real customer can justify a lot of website work.
- Pratfall honesty: "I am not a big agency" can build trust if paired with professionalism and clear next steps.
- Scarcity only if true. "I am only making a few previews this week" is fine if true. Fake urgency is not.

## Night-Before Prep For Each Lead

Write this down before dialing:

- Business: `[business_name]`
- Person, if known: `[owner_or_manager]`
- Category: `[plumbing / auto repair / accounting / roofing / etc.]`
- City: `[city]`
- Public phone: `[phone]`
- Rating/reviews: `[rating] stars, [review_count] reviews`
- Website status: `[none / social-only / basic / broken / uncertain]`
- One honest compliment: `[great reviews / many years open / strong photos / clear specialty]`
- One website issue: `[no site listed / Facebook only / broken site / slow site / directory page]`
- One business outcome: `[more calls / quote requests / appointment bookings / trust]`
- Next step you want: `[5-minute preview call / permission to email preview / scheduled meeting]`

## The Best First-Call Structure

The call should not start with a pitch. It should start with identification, relevance, and permission.

1. Identify yourself.
2. Prove the call is not random.
3. Ask for the right person.
4. State the issue in one sentence.
5. Ask a question.
6. Offer a free preview.
7. Book the next step.

Target length if they are busy: 45 to 90 seconds.

## Core Owner Script

Use this when the owner or decision maker answers.

```text
You: Hi, is this [business_name]?

Prospect: Yes.

You: My name is [your_name]. I know this is out of the blue. I am an independent web designer, and I was looking at local [category] businesses in [city]. I noticed [specific_observation: you have strong Google reviews / you have [review_count] reviews / you look busy], but I could not find a real website listed for you.

Prospect: Okay.

You: I am not calling to sell you something complicated. I am making a few free homepage previews for local businesses this week. The idea is simple: when someone finds you on Google, they should land on a page you control, with your services, photos, service area, and a click-to-call button.

Quick question: are you the person who handles the website or online presence?
```

If yes:

```text
You: Perfect. Would it be useful if I put together a simple preview for [business_name] and showed it to you? No cost or commitment. If you like it, we can talk about turning it into a real site. If not, no problem.
```

If they ask "what do you want from me?":

```text
You: Just five minutes to see if this is worth your attention. I already have the public info I need. I would only want to confirm your main services and best email so I do not build the wrong thing.
```

Close:

```text
You: Would [day/time] or [day/time] be better for a quick look?
```

## Gatekeeper Script

Use this when a receptionist, dispatcher, or staff member answers.

```text
You: Hi, my name is [your_name]. I am trying to reach whoever handles the website or online presence for [business_name]. Is that usually the owner or manager?

Gatekeeper: What is this regarding?

You: I noticed [business_name] has strong Google visibility, but I could not find a real website listed. I am an independent web designer, and I wanted to offer a free preview of a simple site. Not a billing issue, not an emergency. Who would be the best person to ask?
```

If they offer email:

```text
You: That works. What should I put in the subject line so it is recognizable?
```

If they say owner is busy:

```text
You: Totally understand. When is usually the least bad time to catch them for 60 seconds?
```

If they ask to take a message:

```text
You: Sure. Please tell them [your_name], an independent web designer, called because I noticed the business may not have a proper website listed, and I wanted to show a free preview. My number is [your_phone].
```

## No-Website Script

Use when the lead has no website listed.

```text
You: The reason I called is that your Google listing looks active, but I could not find a website that you control. That matters because people who are already searching for [category] in [city] often want to check services, photos, prices or estimates, and whether you serve their area before they call.

I am making a few simple previews this week. For your business, I would build a clean page with your services, service area, reviews, photos if available, and a big click-to-call button.

Would you be open to seeing a preview? You would not owe anything unless you decide it is useful.
```

## Social-Only Script

Use when they only have Facebook, Instagram, Linktree, Yelp, or similar as their main presence.

```text
You: I saw that your online presence seems to be mostly [Facebook/Instagram/Yelp]. That is useful, but it is not the same as owning a simple website. A lot of customers just want to know: do you do the service I need, do you cover my area, can I trust you, and how do I call?

What I would show you is not a big redesign. It is a simple site that supports the social page and turns search traffic into calls.

Would it be worth a quick look if I made a free preview for [business_name]?
```

## Basic-Site Or Broken-Site Script

Use when they have an old Google Business site, parked domain, broken SSL, slow page, directory-only page, or obvious placeholder.

```text
You: I found what looks like a website for [business_name], but it seems [broken / outdated / like a placeholder / hard to use on mobile]. I could be wrong, but if I saw it that way, some customers probably do too.

For local service businesses, the website does not need to be fancy. It needs to load fast, look trustworthy on a phone, explain the services, and make calling easy.

Would you be open to seeing a cleaner version? I can put together a preview before we talk price.
```

## Discovery Questions

Ask no more than three on a cold call.

- "What is the main service you wish more people called you for?"
- "Do most new customers find you from Google, referrals, or repeat business?"
- "Do you serve only [city], or nearby towns too?"
- "If someone lands on a website for you, what is the first thing they should know?"
- "Do you prefer calls, texts, or form requests from new customers?"
- "Are there jobs you do not want more of?"

## Micro-Close Options

Use one of these depending on their energy.

```text
Would it be useful if I made the preview and sent it over?
```

```text
Would you be against taking a quick look if I keep it simple?
```

```text
If I can show you a version that looks professional and makes calling easier, would that be worth a 10-minute conversation?
```

```text
I do not want to waste your time. Should I send a preview, or should I leave it alone?
```

## Price Script

Do not lead with price. If they ask, answer directly.

```text
Prospect: How much does this cost?

You: It depends on scope, but I will be transparent. A simple one-page local business site usually starts around $900 to $1,500. A fuller service site with multiple pages is usually closer to $2,000 to $4,000. Hosting and small updates can be monthly if you want that handled.

But I do not want to quote you blind. My suggestion is: let me show you the preview first. If you like the direction, I will give you a fixed written price before you pay anything.
```

If they say too expensive:

```text
You: That is fair. I would look at it against the value of one real customer. If one good job is worth [rough job value], the question is whether a better web presence can help you win one or two more of those over time. If that math does not work for you, I would not push it.
```

## "We Already Have Someone" Script

```text
Prospect: We already have someone.

You: That makes sense. If they are actively handling it, I do not want to step on that. The only reason I called is that from the outside, I saw [specific issue]. Do you know if they are already fixing that, or is it just not a priority right now?
```

If they say it is being fixed:

```text
You: Got it. I will leave it alone. If you ever want a second set of eyes, I am happy to take a look.
```

If they say not a priority:

```text
You: Understood. Would it be useful if I sent a simple preview anyway? You can compare it with what you have, and there is no obligation.
```

## "Send Me Information" Script

This usually means "go away" unless you turn it into a specific next step.

```text
Prospect: Just send me information.

You: I can. To make sure I send something useful, should I send a generic note, or should I send a preview specific to [business_name]?
```

If preview:

```text
You: Perfect. What is the best email? And if I send it by [day], should I call you the next day or leave it with you?
```

If generic note:

```text
You: No problem. I will keep it short. If it is not useful, you can ignore it.
```

## "Not Interested" Script

```text
Prospect: Not interested.

You: No problem. Before I let you go, is that because the website is already handled, or because you are not looking to spend on it right now?
```

If handled:

```text
You: Makes sense. I will mark that and not bother you.
```

If money/timing:

```text
You: Fair. I will not push. If I send a free preview with no payment request, would that still be annoying, or useful for later?
```

If they still say no:

```text
You: Understood. I will leave it alone. Have a good day.
```

## Busy-Now Script

```text
Prospect: I am busy.

You: I figured you might be. I can be very quick. I noticed [specific issue] and wanted to offer a free website preview. Should I call back later today, or is there a better email to send it to?
```

## Skeptical Owner Script

```text
Prospect: How did you get my number?

You: From your public business listing. I am only calling the business number, and if you do not want calls like this, I will mark that and stop.
```

```text
Prospect: Is this a scam?

You: Fair question. No. I am not asking for payment or personal information on this call. I am an independent web designer. The only thing I am asking is whether I can show you a free preview. If you like it, I would send a written scope and invoice before anything happens.
```

```text
Prospect: Are you with Google?

You: No. I am not with Google. I found your public listing on Google, but I am an independent designer.
```

## If They Ask About The Company

Because nothing is registered yet, stay clean.

```text
Prospect: What company are you with?

You: Right now it is just me as an independent web designer. I am taking on a few local clients before turning this into anything bigger. If we work together, I will give you a written scope, invoice, and clear approval process before payment.
```

If there are two of you:

```text
You: Right now it is just two of us working independently. We are not pretending to be a big agency. We are building a small client base by doing the work directly and keeping the process simple.
```

## Voicemail

Keep it under 20 seconds.

```text
Hi, this is [your_name]. I am an independent web designer. I was looking at [business_name] in [city] and could not find a proper website listed, even though your Google reviews look strong. I wanted to offer a free quick preview of what a simple site could look like. My number is [your_phone]. Again, [your_name], [your_phone].
```

## Follow-Up After Sending Preview

```text
You: Hi [name], it is [your_name]. I sent over the quick preview for [business_name]. Did you get a chance to open it, or should I resend it?

Prospect: I saw it.

You: Great. What was your honest reaction? Is it in the right direction, or did I miss the way you want the business presented?
```

If positive:

```text
You: Good. The next step would be simple: I clean it up, confirm the services and service area with you, then give you a fixed price to launch it. If the price works, I send a written scope and invoice. Would you want me to price the starter version or the fuller version?
```

If neutral:

```text
You: That is useful. What would need to change for it to feel like your business?
```

If negative:

```text
You: Fair. I would rather hear that now. Is the issue the design, the services shown, or that a website is not a priority?
```

## Close For A Paid Customer

Do not close with pressure. Close with a clear next step.

```text
You: Based on what you told me, I think the starter version is enough: homepage, services, service area, photos, reviews, click-to-call, and a contact form. I can do that for [fixed_price], with [hosting/update terms if any].

If you want to move forward, I will send a written scope today. You approve it, pay the invoice, and I start. If you do not like the scope, we stop there.

Do you want me to send that over?
```

## Best Niches And Angles

Use these because the lead platform has historically treated them as practical early opportunities.

- Plumbing, HVAC, electrical, roofing, pest control, cleaning, landscaping: "more calls and quote requests."
- Contractors and home services: "show service area, photos, insurance/license info if true, and request-estimate flow."
- Auto repair: "trust, services, location, hours, click-to-call, common repairs."
- Accounting/tax: "credibility, services, appointment booking, seasonal urgency."
- Dental, med spa, veterinary, fitness: "booking, trust, before/after or service pages where compliant."
- Restaurants are lower priority unless the site is extremely bad because website budgets are often smaller.

## What Not To Say

- "We guarantee more customers."
- "We work with Google."
- "Your website is terrible."
- "You are losing thousands every month." Say "could be costing calls" unless you have proof.
- "This offer expires today" unless it really does.
- "I already built your website" if you only made a preview.
- "I am calling about your Google listing" in a way that implies official affiliation.
- "Can I text you?" unless you first get clear permission.
